# 2. A Brief History of AI

AI did not appear overnight. It grew out of centuries of people building machines to **organize data**, then to **run programs**, and eventually to **learn patterns and generate content**.

---

## Eras before modern AI

### Era of Tabulation

Researchers call these centuries the **Era of Tabulation** — a time when machines helped humans **sort data into structures** to reveal its secrets.

Think: counting, organizing, tables, early mechanical aids for processing information at scale.

### Era of Programming

Next came the **Era of Programming**. Scientists built electronic computers that could run more than one kind of instruction — today we call those **programs** — and thus do more than one kind of calculation.

Example: the **Electronic Numerical Integrator and Computer (ENIAC)** at the University of Pennsylvania.

Programmable computers later guided astronauts from Earth to the Moon and were **reprogrammed during Apollo 13** to bring the crew safely home — a vivid reminder that flexible software on powerful hardware changes what machines can do.

---

## Timeline highlights

### 1940s — Turing machines

**Alan Turing** develops the idea of **Turing machines** — a foundational model of computation. This theoretical groundwork shapes how we think about what machines can compute.

---

### 1955–1956 — The birth of “artificial intelligence”

| Year | Event |
|------|--------|
| **1955** | **John McCarthy** coins the term **“artificial intelligence”** at the first AI conference at **Dartmouth College**. |
| **1955** | Later that year, **Allen Newell**, **J.C. Shaw**, and **Herbert Simon** create the **Logic Theorist** — often cited as the first running AI software program. |
| **1956** | Logic Theorist is recognized as an early program for **automated reasoning**. |

McCarthy would later invent the **Lisp** language, important in early AI research.

The Dartmouth researchers proposed that:

> “Every aspect of learning or any other feature of intelligence can be so precisely described that a machine can be made to simulate it.”

They named that vision **artificial intelligence** and raised significant funding, aiming to achieve major progress within about **20 years**.

**Logic Theorist** proved theorems at a strong level and successfully proved **38 of the first 52 theorems** in chapter 2 of *Principia Mathematica*.

---

### 1995 — The standard textbook

**Stuart Russell** and **Peter Norvig** publish ***Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach***.

It becomes the most commonly used AI textbook worldwide (used at more than **1,500** institutions).

---

### 1997 — Deep Blue vs Kasparov

**IBM Deep Blue** beats then world chess champion **Garry Kasparov** in a match (and rematch).

A landmark for AI in a constrained, high-prestige domain: search + evaluation at massive scale.

---

### 2005 — Self-driving in the desert

A **Stanford University** robot drives itself down a **131-mile** desert trail — an early public milestone for autonomous vehicles.

---

### 2011 — Watson on Jeopardy!

**IBM Watson** beats champions **Ken Jennings** and **Brad Rutter** on the US game show **Jeopardy!**

Shows AI handling messy natural-language clues, not only clean board-game rules.

---

### 2016 — AlphaGo

**DeepMind’s AlphaGo**, powered by a **deep neural network**, defeats **Lee Sedol** (18 Go world titles) in a five-game match.

Go has simple rules but enormous complexity — far more possible moves and board positions than chess. DeepMind was later purchased by **Google**.

---

### 2020 — GPT-3

**OpenAI** introduces **GPT-3**, a language model that uses deep learning to generate code, poetry, and other writing that can be exceptionally similar to human-authored text.

---

### 2022 — ChatGPT

**ChatGPT** debuts in **November 2022** (OpenAI), initially built on the **GPT-3** LLM family.

By **January 2023**, ChatGPT has more than **100 million** users — one of the fastest consumer adoptions of a software product in history.

---

### 2023 — Multimodal GPT-4, Bard, Gemini

| When | Event |
|------|--------|
| **March 2023** | OpenAI releases **GPT-4** — generally seen as a step up from GPT-3.5; **multimodal** (images + text). |
| **March 2023** | Google releases **Bard**, based on **LaMDA** and **PaLM** LLMs. |
| **December 2023** | Google releases the **Gemini** large language model — successor to LaMDA and PaLM 2. |

---

### 2024 — Longer context and text-to-video

| When | Event |
|------|--------|
| **February 2024** | Google releases **Gemini 1.5** (beta), able to run up to about **1 million tokens** consistently. |
| **Same day** | OpenAI announces plans for **Sora**, a generative model for **text-to-video**. |

---

## How to read this history

| Era / phase | What machines mainly did |
|-------------|---------------------------|
| Tabulation | Sort and structure data |
| Programming | Run flexible instructions / calculations |
| Classical AI | Rules, search, theorem proving, games |
| Deep learning era | Learn from huge datasets; vision, speech, Go |
| Generative AI era | Produce text, code, images, video; chat products |

Each step builds on the last: better hardware, more data, better algorithms — and a shift from **hand-written rules** toward **learned models**, then toward **generative** systems you use every day.

---

## Key takeaway

AI’s story runs from **tabulating data**, through **programmable computers**, to **Dartmouth’s 1950s vision**, game-changing demos (**Deep Blue**, **Watson**, **AlphaGo**), and today’s **LLMs and chatbots** (**GPT-3/4**, **ChatGPT**, **Gemini**). The field is old in idea, young in the form that feels mainstream now.
