# 1.1 Introduction — What is Artificial Intelligence?

> “Artificial intelligence is a branch of computer science that enables machines to perform tasks that typically require human intelligence.”

---

## Simple definition

**Artificial intelligence (AI)** means making computers and machines do things that usually need human intelligence — such as learning, problem-solving, and decision-making. In short: teaching machines to perform intelligent-looking tasks.

A more formal angle:

- AI combines **computer science** and **robust datasets** to enable problem-solving.
- AI refers to a machine’s ability to **learn patterns** and **make predictions**.
- AI does **not** replace human decisions. It **adds value** to human judgment.

---

## A more detailed breakdown

### Learning

AI systems can learn from data and improve performance over time.

### Problem-solving

They can analyze situations, identify patterns, and suggest solutions.

### Decision-making

AI can make choices based on what it has learned and analyzed.

**Everyday examples**

- Voice assistants (Siri, Alexa)
- Recommendation systems on websites
- Self-driving cars

### How it works (high level)

AI uses **algorithms** and **data** to train **models**, so systems can perform tasks without needing an explicit human instruction for every single case.

---

## Key takeaway

AI is a field of computer science focused on intelligent behavior in machines — especially pattern learning and prediction — that supports people rather than replacing their judgment.
